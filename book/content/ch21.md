::: {.synopsis data-title="Synopsis"}
You have purified protein. Three independent claims are now in play, and they must not be conflated. **Folded**: the chain adopts a defined, compact structure. **As-designed**: that structure matches the model you built. **Functional**: it does the chemistry. This chapter equips the first two. SEC-MALS gives oligomeric state and absolute mass (21.1); CD reports secondary-structure content and compares it to the design (21.2); DSF/nanoDSF/DSC give a Tm that no predictor told you (21.3); SAXS gives a low-resolution solution shape (21.4); and 21.5 insists that none of these prove function.
:::

## 21.1 SEC-MALS and Oligomeric State

Ordinary SEC (Chapter 20) reports *hydrodynamic size*, which depends on shape, and assigns mass only through a shape-dependent calibration — so an elongated monomer can masquerade as a globular dimer. **SEC-MALS** (size-exclusion chromatography coupled to multi-angle light scattering) removes that ambiguity by measuring **absolute molar mass** directly, independent of shape and of the column calibration.

The instrument places a **MALS detector** (e.g., Wyatt DAWN) and a **differential refractive-index (dRI)** detector in line after the SEC column. Light scattered by a macromolecule is proportional to the product of its concentration and its molar mass; the dRI signal gives concentration (via the protein's refractive-index increment dn/dc, ≈ 0.185 mL/g for typical proteins), and the angular dependence of scattering gives mass and, for larger species, radius of gyration. Dividing the scattering by the concentration at each elution slice yields a mass that is *flat across a monodisperse peak* — a horizontal mass trace overlaid on the chromatographic peak is the signature of a homogeneous, single-species sample. A sloping or stepped mass across the peak reveals self-association equilibria or unresolved species. SEC-MALS is the rigorous answer to "is my design the monomer I designed, or has it dimerized?" — a question SEC alone cannot settle, and one that bears directly on whether an apparent active site even exists as drawn.

## 21.2 Circular Dichroism and Secondary-Structure Match

**Circular dichroism (CD)** spectroscopy measures the differential absorption of left- versus right-circularly-polarized light by chiral chromophores. In the **far-UV** region (~190–260 nm), the relevant chromophore is the **peptide backbone amide**, and its CD signal is exquisitely sensitive to backbone dihedral angles — that is, to secondary structure. Far-UV CD is therefore the fastest, cheapest experimental test of whether a design has the **secondary-structure content** it was built to have.

The spectral signatures are diagnostic. An **α-helix** gives a double minimum at **208 nm and 222 nm** and a strong maximum near 192 nm. A **β-sheet** gives a single, broader minimum near **217 nm** and a maximum near 195 nm. A **random coil / unfolded** chain gives a strong minimum near 198 nm and little signal above 210 nm. Real proteins are mixtures, and deconvolution algorithms (BeStSel, DichroWeb's CONTIN/SELCON/CDSSTR) estimate fractional helix/sheet/coil from the spectrum. The power of CD for design validation is comparison: take the secondary-structure fractions predicted from your design model (count helix and sheet residues, or run DSSP on the model) and compare them to the experimental deconvolution. A design built as a four-helix bundle that gives a clean 208/222 nm double-minimum with ~70% helix is *consistent with* the model; one that gives a 217 nm β-minimum or a 198 nm coil signature has folded into something else, or not folded at all.

CD also reports stability. Monitoring the CD signal at a fixed wavelength (222 nm for helices) while ramping temperature gives a **thermal melt** whose midpoint is the Tm — a complementary route to the methods in 21.3.

::: {.worked data-title="Worked Example 21.1 · Interpreting a CD spectrum vs. the design model"}
**Problem.** Your design model is a five-helix bundle; DSSP on the model assigns 78% of residues to α-helix and the rest to loops, with no β-strand. The purified protein's far-UV CD spectrum shows minima at **209 nm** and **221 nm** of comparable depth, a steep positive band below 200 nm, and a deconvolution (BeStSel) reporting **64% helix, 4% sheet, 32% other**. Mean residue ellipticity at 222 nm is **−24,000 deg·cm²·dmol⁻¹**. Does the protein fold as designed?

**Solution.** The **209/221 nm double minimum** is the textbook α-helical signature, and the [θ]₂₂₂ of −24,000 corresponds to roughly 65–70% helix by the canonical estimate (% helix ≈ [θ]₂₂₂ / −33,000 × 100, i.e., ≈ 73% if fully helical reference is −33,000; using −30,000 gives ≈ 80%) — consistent with the deconvolution's 64%. The design wanted ~78% helix; the experiment gives ~64%, with a small spurious 4% sheet. **Verdict: folded, predominantly helical, broadly consistent with the design** — but with modestly *less* helix than modeled, suggesting some terminal or loop fraying. CD confirms the *secondary-structure class* and approximate content; it cannot confirm the *topology* (which helix packs against which) — that requires SAXS (21.4) or a structure (Chapter 23). Report "predominantly α-helical, consistent with the designed secondary-structure content," not "structure confirmed."
:::

## 21.3 DSF, nanoDSF, and DSC for Thermal Stability

A folded protein is not necessarily a *stable* one, and stability is something **no structure predictor reported**. pLDDT, PAE, and a confident AlphaFold model say nothing about the Tm you are about to measure — confidence in a predicted *shape* is not a prediction of *thermodynamic stability*. Three thermal methods, in increasing rigor and cost, supply it.

**Differential scanning fluorimetry (DSF)**, or the **thermal shift assay**, is the cheapest and highest-throughput. It uses an extrinsic dye (SYPRO Orange) that fluoresces when bound to hydrophobic surfaces. As temperature rises and the protein unfolds, its buried hydrophobic core becomes exposed, the dye binds, and fluorescence jumps; the inflection of the melt curve is the apparent **Tm**. DSF runs in a standard **qPCR instrument** in 96- or 384-well format, needs micrograms of protein, and is ideal for screening many designs or buffer conditions at once. Its limitation: the dye can bind exposed hydrophobic patches even in folded designs (giving high baseline signal or no clean transition), and it reports an *apparent* Tm.

**nanoDSF** (e.g., NanoTemper Prometheus) is dye-free. It tracks the **intrinsic tryptophan/tyrosine fluorescence** ratio (350/330 nm), which shifts as aromatic residues move from a buried to a solvent-exposed environment on unfolding. It needs only a few microliters in capillaries, simultaneously monitors aggregation by back-reflection (light scattering), and gives a cleaner, label-free Tm. It is the recommended default for characterizing a small set of purified designs.

**Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC)** directly measures the **excess heat capacity** as the protein unfolds, yielding not just the Tm but the calorimetric enthalpy ΔH_cal of unfolding and, with a model, the van't Hoff enthalpy — i.e., the actual thermodynamics. It needs more material (sub-milligram amounts at ~0.5–1 mg/mL) and is lower-throughput, but it is the gold standard when thermodynamic rigor matters.

::: {.method data-title="Method 21.1 · A nanoDSF thermal melt (Prometheus-style)"}
1. **Prepare sample.** Dialyze or buffer-exchange the purified protein into the assay buffer (avoid imidazole and high glycerol). Adjust to **0.2–1 mg/mL**; ~10 µL per capillary is enough.
2. **Load capillaries.** Dip standard-grade capillaries into each sample to fill by capillarity; seat them on the holder. Include a buffer-only and, if testing stabilizers, a condition series.
3. **Set the scan.** Program a temperature ramp, typically **20 → 95 °C at 1 °C/min**. Choose excitation power so initial 330 and 350 nm counts are in range (not saturated).
4. **Acquire.** The instrument records intrinsic fluorescence at 330 and 350 nm and the back-reflection (scattering) signal continuously during the ramp.
5. **Compute Tm.** The software plots the **350/330 ratio** vs. temperature; the **inflection point (peak of the first derivative)** is the Tm. The onset of the scattering signal gives the aggregation onset temperature (T_agg).
6. **Replicate and report.** Run at least duplicate capillaries; report Tm ± SD, note whether unfolding is reversible (re-scan), and flag any aggregation onset *below* the Tm — a sign the design unfolds by aggregating rather than by clean two-state melting.
:::

## 21.4 SAXS and Solution Shape

CD reports secondary-structure content but not topology; SEC-MALS reports mass but not shape. **Small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS)** fills the gap by reporting the **low-resolution solution shape and size** of the particle, in solution, at near-physiological conditions, without crystals. A monodisperse protein solution is exposed to a collimated X-ray beam, and the scattered intensity I(q) is recorded as a function of momentum transfer q. Two model-free quantities come almost immediately: the **radius of gyration (Rg)** from the low-q **Guinier region** (ln I(q) vs. q² is linear, with slope −Rg²/3), and the **maximum particle dimension (Dmax)** and **pair-distance distribution P(r)** from the full curve. The shape of P(r) is itself diagnostic — a symmetric bell indicates a globular particle, a skewed tail an elongated one, and multiple peaks a multidomain or dumbbell shape.

For design validation, the decisive test is the **Kratky plot** (I(q)·q² vs. q): a folded, globular protein gives a bell-shaped peak that returns toward baseline, whereas an unfolded or highly flexible chain rises monotonically. Beyond folded-vs-unfolded, you compute the *theoretical* scattering profile of your design model (CRYSOL, FoXS) and overlay it on the experimental I(q); the goodness of fit (χ²) quantifies how well the design's overall shape matches the protein in solution. SAXS thus elevates the claim from "folded and helical" (CD) to "folded into a particle of the size and gross shape I designed" — short of a structure, it is the strongest solution-state evidence that a design is *as-designed*.

::: {.toolbox data-title="Tool Box 21.1 · SEC-MALS and SAXS analysis"}
**ASTRA** (Wyatt, v8) — vendor software for SEC-MALS; computes absolute molar mass per elution slice from MALS + dRI (set dn/dc ≈ 0.185 mL/g).
**ATSAS suite** (EMBL, v3.2) — the standard SAXS toolkit: *PRIMUS* (Guinier/Rg, processing), *GNOM* (P(r), Dmax), *CRYSOL* (model → theoretical I(q) and χ² to data), *DAMMIF/DAMMIN* (ab initio bead models).
**FoXS / MultiFoXS** (web/standalone) — fast model-to-SAXS fitting, including flexible/multi-state ensembles.
**BeStSel** (web, 2018) — far-UV CD secondary-structure deconvolution, robust for β-rich proteins; **DichroWeb** wraps CONTIN/SELCON3/CDSSTR.
**PR.ThermControl / PR.Stability** (NanoTemper) — nanoDSF acquisition and Tm/T_agg analysis.
:::

## 21.5 Separating Folding from Function

The discipline of this chapter is refusing to let three claims collapse into one. The methods above establish, at most, that a design is **folded** (compact, defined secondary structure — CD, Kratky) and **as-designed in gross shape and mass** (SEC-MALS, SAXS χ², CD content matching the model). They say *nothing* about **function**. A protein can fold beautifully into exactly the intended bundle and have no catalytic activity whatsoever — because the active-site geometry is subtly wrong, a catalytic residue is rotated, the pocket is occluded, or the transition state is simply not stabilized. Conversely, weak activity in a poorly behaved prep can be an artifact of a contaminating host enzyme, not the design (Chapter 22 is entirely about ruling this out).

The correct logical chain is sequential and each link is a separate experiment. *Is it folded?* — CD and a clean SEC/Kratky. *Is it the right oligomeric state and size?* — SEC-MALS. *Is its solution shape consistent with the model?* — SAXS. *Is it stable enough to assay?* — Tm by nanoDSF/DSC. Only after these do you ask *does it catalyze?* (Chapter 22) and finally *does its structure match the design at atomic resolution, including the active site?* (Chapter 23). Reporting "folded" when you have only a single CD scan, or "as-designed" when you have only mass, or — worst — "functional" when you have only folding data, are the errors that erode trust in design papers. State each claim at the level the data support, and no higher.

::: {.conceptcheck data-title="Concept Check 21.1"}
A design gives a flat SEC-MALS mass at the monomer value, a clean α-helical CD spectrum matching the model's helix content, a SAXS Kratky plot with a clear globular peak and CRYSOL χ² = 1.3 to the model, and a nanoDSF Tm of 71 °C. A colleague writes that the experiments "confirm the design is a functional enzyme." Which word is unsupported, and what single class of experiment is missing?
:::

::: {.reality data-title="Reality Check 21.1 · The well-scored design that aggregates"}
A common and humbling outcome: a backbone with excellent designability metrics — high AlphaFold pLDDT, low PAE, a Rosetta energy in the favorable tail, a predicted Tm from a stability model in the 60s — expresses solubly, runs as a clean monomer on analytical SEC, and then *aggregates* the moment you concentrate it for SAXS or DSC, or melts with an aggregation onset *below* its Tm. None of the *in silico* scores predicted this, because they scored an idealized static model and not the kinetic and colloidal reality of a real solution. Across reported campaigns it is routine for a substantial fraction of well-scored, well-expressed designs to fail at the biophysical stage for stability or aggregation reasons that no current predictor reliably anticipates. The lesson is not that the scores are useless — they enrich the shortlist enormously — but that *the Tm and the Kratky plot are measurements, not predictions*, and a design is not "validated" until they exist.
:::

The design models and their DSSP-derived secondary-structure fractions are provided.

For each design, produce a characterization report that:

- States the oligomeric state and whether the SEC-MALS mass trace is flat across the peak (monodisperse) or sloping (associating); compute the oligomer number from the absolute mass.

## Chapter Summary

- **21.1** SEC-MALS gives shape-independent *absolute* molar mass; a flat mass trace across the peak is the signature of a monodisperse, defined oligomeric state — which SEC alone cannot establish.
- **21.2** Far-UV CD reports secondary-structure content via the backbone amide; the 208/222 nm helix double-minimum, the 217 nm sheet minimum, and the 198 nm coil minimum let you compare experiment to the model's content.
- **21.3** DSF (cheap, high-throughput, dye-based), nanoDSF (dye-free, label-free, the practical default), and DSC (thermodynamic gold standard) measure the Tm — a quantity no structure predictor reported.
- **21.4** SAXS gives solution-state Rg, Dmax, and shape; the Kratky plot distinguishes folded from unfolded, and CRYSOL/FoXS χ² quantifies agreement between the model and the particle in solution.
- **21.5** Folded, as-designed, and functional are three distinct claims established by three distinct experiments; report each only at the level its data support.
