# Preface

Enzyme design has crossed a threshold. For most of its history the field could generate plausible active sites but rarely working catalysts; the gap between a confident computer model and a measurable turnover number was where designs went to die. In the last few years that gap has narrowed dramatically. Atom-level generative models now build protein backbones directly around a specified catalytic geometry, and de novo enzymes rivaling natural rates have been reported for hydrolases and metalloenzymes without resorting to directed evolution. This book is written for the moment after that threshold — for readers who want not just to admire the results but to produce them.

The book makes three commitments. The first is **catalysis before computation**: the chemistry, not the neural network, is the bottleneck. A designer who cannot draw the transition state and name the functional groups that stabilize it will not be rescued by a larger model. The second is that **a design is a hypothesis until the assay says otherwise** — every computational claim is provisional until protein is expressed, folding is confirmed, and turnover is measured against the right controls. The third is that **failure is curriculum**: most designs fail, and the patterns of failure are the most reliable teacher the field has.

**Audience.** The book is written for graduate students and working scientists — computational biologists who need the wet-lab half of the story, and experimentalists who want to drive the software rather than outsource it. It assumes undergraduate biochemistry and comfort with Python, and it teaches the rest.

**What this book deliberately omits.** It is not a software manual; specific commands age in months, so tools are taught conceptually and version-stamped in Tool Boxes. It is not a survey of every generative model; it teaches a representative spine and the judgment to absorb successors. And it does not pretend the success rates are higher than they are.

**Three reading paths.** The *Theory track* (a lecture course) follows Parts I–III and Chapter 12. The *Practitioner track* runs a self-contained design campaign through Part IV and the capstone in Appendix E. The *Seminar track* is paper-driven, anchored by the journal-club Studio Exercises in Chapters 5 and 11 and the case studies in Chapter 26. Each chapter is built from the same template so that, whichever path you take, you always know what you are looking at.
