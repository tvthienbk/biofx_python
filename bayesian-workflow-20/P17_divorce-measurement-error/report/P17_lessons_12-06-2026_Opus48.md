# Lessons Report — Project 17: Divorce (error-in-variables + Student-t)

**Author:** Truong Van Thien, PhD · **Date:** 12-06-2026 · **Model tag:** Opus48

## 1. The concept taught
**Measurement error (error-in-variables).** State divorce rates are estimates, and small states estimate
them noisily — the dataset even ships the standard errors. Instead of treating the observed rate as truth,
we model it as a noisy reading of a **latent true rate**: `D_obs ~ Normal(D_true, SE)`, with the regression
acting on `D_true`. The payoff is **measurement-error shrinkage**: noisy states are pulled toward the fitted
line in proportion to their SE. We also add a **Student-t** robust variant and compare it by LOO.

## 2. Workflow lessons from this run (with the actual numbers)
1. **Mind the column names:** the SE columns are `Divorce SE` / `Marriage SE` **with spaces** — renamed to
   `Divorce_SE` / `Marriage_SE` before use. A silent KeyError trap if you assume dots.
2. **Recovery first:** simulating `a=0, bA=−0.6, bM=0.3, sigma=0.6` recovered all four inside their 90% HDIs.
3. **Age at marriage matters, marriage rate does not:** **bA = −0.616** (HDI [−0.928, −0.328], excludes 0);
   **bM = +0.053** (HDI crosses 0).
4. **Shrinkage scales with SE:** mean |observed − latent| is **0.443 for high-SE states** vs **0.128 for
   low-SE states** — the noisiest small states (SD, AK, WY) shrink ~3.5× harder toward the line.
5. **The robust variant barely helps here:** Student-t vs Normal LOO `elpd_diff` = **0.48 ± 1.03 = 0.46 SE**
   (indistinguishable); the Student-t **nu ≈ 19** says the tails are essentially Gaussian. (Max Pareto-k̂ = 1.26
   flags a few influential states, so LOO is approximate.)
6. **Calibrated (SBC p = 0.419).**

## 3. Common mistakes
- **Assuming the SE columns are `Divorce_SE`** — they have spaces; rename first.
- **Treating the observed divorce rate as the truth** — that ignores that small states are measured noisily.
- **Forgetting to put the SE on the standardized scale** when you standardize the outcome.
- **Expecting Student-t to always win.** Here nu ≈ 19 and LOO is a tie — the data do not need heavy tails.
