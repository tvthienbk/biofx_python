# Lessons Report — Project 12: Radon (varying-intercept multilevel) + LOGO-CV

**Author:** Truong Van Thien, PhD · **Date:** 12-06-2026 · **Model tag:** Opus48

## 1. The concept taught
The canonical **multilevel regression**: a varying intercept per county, a group-level predictor (soil
uranium), and the **pooling spectrum** (no / complete / partial). Three craft skills: the **non-centered**
parameterization to dodge the funnel, **shrinkage** of small counties toward the uranium-informed line, and
the honest cross-validation for grouped data — **leave-one-GROUP-out CV (LOGO)** versus naive leave-one-row
LOO. Tier 3 adds **SBC** to certify calibration.

## 2. Workflow lessons from this run (with the actual numbers)
1. **Recovery first:** simulating with `g0=1.4, g_u=0.7, b_floor=−0.65, sigma_a=0.35, sigma=0.75` recovered all five inside their 90% HDIs; R̂ = 1.00.
2. **Non-centering works:** the fit had **0 divergences**, R̂ = 1.00, ESS ≥ 1169 — no funnel.
3. **The floor effect:** `b_floor = −0.636` (94% HDI −0.764 to −0.515) → **×0.53** on the radon scale; first floors have about half the radon of basements.
4. **Uranium explains county baselines:** `g_u = 0.267` (HDI 0.204 to 0.333) per +1 SD county log-uranium.
5. **Shrinkage is visible:** the three single-home counties move from raw means 2.50/1.39/2.24 toward the grand mean **1.26** — small-n counties are tempered.
6. **LOGO is the honest CV:** naive LOO scored **−1.060** per home but LOGO scored **−1.075** per home on the same 382 homes — naive LOO was optimistic by **5.8 elpd** because it had seen other homes of each county.
7. **SBC certifies calibration:** rank histogram for `b_floor` flat, chi-square p = **0.806** (100 sims, 349 s, within budget).

## 3. Common mistakes
- **Using a centered varying intercept** without checking divergences — the funnel returns.
- **Reporting naive LOO as new-county accuracy.** For hierarchical data the prediction target dictates the CV scheme; new-county prediction needs LOGO.
- **Over-trusting small-n county estimates.** With one home, the county estimate is almost entirely the uranium model — say so.
- **Forgetting that the group predictor is constant within county** — uranium is a *county* covariate, not a home covariate.

## 4. Try it yourself
1. Add a **varying floor slope** per county; does the floor effect vary across counties?
2. Run LOGO on the **3 single-home counties**; why is per-home elpd so different from naive LOO there?
3. Drop the uranium predictor; watch `sigma_a` (unexplained county variation) grow.
