# Lessons Report — Project 06: Switching wells ~ arsenic + distance

**Author:** Truong Van Thien, PhD · **Date:** 12-06-2026 · **Model tag:** Opus48

## 1. The concept taught
The first **GLM**: a logistic regression `logit(p) = a + b_ars·arsenic_z + b_dist·dist_z` for whether a
Bangladeshi household switched away from an arsenic-contaminated well. Three skills carry forward: the
**logit link** with **odds-ratio** (`exp(coef)`) interpretation, the **prior predictive on the
probability scale**, and a **binary PPC** via a calibration plot.

## 2. Workflow lessons from this run (with the actual numbers)
1. **Recovery first:** simulating with a = 0.3, b_ars = 0.9, b_dist = −0.6 recovered all three (0.379,
   0.932, −0.703) inside their 90% HDIs before any real data.
2. **Prior predictive must be on the probability scale:** pushing the linear predictor through `invlogit`
   gives implied P(switch) with mean 0.48 and only **7% below 0.05 / 7% above 0.95** — spread across
   (0,1), not piled at the edges. A coefficient prior that looks fine on the logit scale can secretly
   force near-certain 0/1 probabilities; only this check reveals it.
3. **The headline result (odds ratios):** arsenic OR = **1.67 per +1 sd** (94% HDI 1.53, 1.82); distance
   OR = **0.71 per +1 sd** (94% HDI 0.66, 0.77). Arsenic is the dominant driver of switching.
4. **Binary PPC + calibration:** observed switch rate 0.575 vs predicted 0.575 (**bayes-p = 0.51**); across
   predicted-probability deciles the max gap is just **0.039** — well calibrated.
5. **Arsenic helps decisively:** LOO favours `dist+arsenic` over `dist` alone by `elpd_diff` = **71.7 ±
   12.1 SE** (≈ 6 SE); stacking weight 0.94; **max Pareto-k̂ = 0.16**, so the comparison is fully reliable.
6. **Prior power-scaling is flat** (E[b_ars] = 0.510 throughout, E[b_dist] = −0.346) — with n = 3020 the
   likelihood dominates entirely.

## 3. Common mistakes
- **Doing the prior predictive on the logit scale**, missing that the implied probabilities pile at 0/1.
- **Interpreting the raw coefficient as a probability change** — it is a change in *log-odds*; exponentiate
  to get the odds ratio, and remember the probability effect depends on the baseline rate.
- **Forgetting to standardize**, so `Normal(0,1)` on the slope is no longer weakly-informative.

## 4. Try it yourself
1. Add the arsenic × distance interaction — does arsenic's effect depend on distance?
2. Add education (`educ4`) and read its odds ratio; does it shift the arsenic coefficient?
3. Replace `arsenic_z` with standardized `log(arsenic)` and compare the calibration plots.

## 5. Self-check
1. Why must the prior predictive for a logistic model be checked on the probability scale, not the logit scale?
2. arsenic coef = +0.510. What is the odds ratio, and what does "per +1 sd" mean here?
3. The calibration plot's max gap is 0.039. What would a poorly calibrated model's plot look like?
4. `elpd_diff` = 71.7 ± 12.1 SE for adding arsenic. Is it worth it? How many SE is that?
5. Power-scaling did not move the coefficients at all. Why does n = 3020 make that unsurprising?
