# Lessons Report — Project 19: Coal-Mining Disasters (Poisson changepoint)

**Author:** Truong Van Thien, PhD · **Date:** 12-06-2026 · **Model tag:** Opus48

## 1. The concept taught
A **discrete-latent changepoint** model — an unknown *year* at which a Poisson rate jumps — and the
**model-aware diagnostics** it demands. Because the switchpoint is discrete, PyMC must use **compound
sampling** (Metropolis for the switch + NUTS for the rates), so the familiar "0 divergences" rule no
longer applies. You then learn to **marginalize** the discrete variable away to recover clean full-NUTS
diagnostics — two views of the same model.

## 2. Workflow lessons from this run (with the actual numbers)
1. **Recovery first:** simulating a known switch at 1900 (rates 3.2 / 0.9) recovered switch ≈ 1902 and
   rates 3.12 / 0.86 before any real analysis.
2. **The result:** the disaster rate dropped at **~1891** (94% HDI 1887–1897) from **3.06 to 0.94 per
   year — a 69% fall**, matching the era of major mining-safety reform.
3. **Diagnostics are model-aware:** divergence count is *not* the criterion here. Instead the discrete
   switchpoint **mixes** (R̂ = 1.00, ESS ≈ 1740) and the rate blocks are pristine (ESS ≈ 7600 / 9200).
4. **Marginalizing restores full NUTS:** summing over the switchpoint analytically gave **0 divergences**
   and identical rate estimates (3.06 / 0.94) — proof the compound result was trustworthy.
5. **The change is real and the model fits:** LOO favours the changepoint over a constant rate by
   **32.0 ± 8.0 (4 SE)**; PPC count statistics all pass (zeros p = 0.38, max p = 0.97); SBC ranks are
   uniform (p = 0.74); power-scaling leaves the rates unmoved.

## 3. Common mistakes
- **Applying the "0 divergences" rule to a compound-step model.** It is meaningless there; judge mixing
  of the discrete variable and per-block R̂/ESS instead.
- **Forgetting the marginalized check.** If the compound and marginalized models disagree, something is
  wrong — they should match.
- **Over-reading a hard switch.** Real safety improvements were gradual; the step is a useful idealization.

## 4. Try it yourself
1. Replace the hard switch with a logistic ramp and estimate the transition width; does LOO prefer it?
2. In Model A, change the Metropolis tuning and watch the switchpoint's ESS — why is mixing the right
   diagnostic for a discrete latent?
3. Add a second changepoint and use LOO to ask whether the data support one change or two.
