# Lessons Report — Project 13: Chimpanzees (correlated varying intercepts + slopes)

**Author:** Truong Van Thien, PhD · **Date:** 12-06-2026 · **Model tag:** Opus48

## 1. The concept taught
The final multilevel ingredient: **correlated varying effects**. Each chimp gets its own intercept *and*
its own slope, drawn jointly from a 2-D distribution whose **correlation** is given a
`pm.LKJCholeskyCov(eta=2)` prior. The craft skills: build the covariance from a Cholesky factor, sample it
**non-centered** (`chol @ z`), and **read off the posterior intercept–slope correlation** with its honest
uncertainty. Tier 3 adds **SBC**.

## 2. Workflow lessons from this run (with the actual numbers)
1. **Recovery first:** simulating with `a_bar=0.4, b_bar=0.5, rho=−0.5` recovered all three (−0.28, 0.73, 0.04) inside their 90% HDIs — though `rho`'s HDI is wide (−0.63 to 0.70), a warning that 7 actors barely identify a correlation.
2. **Non-centered MvNormal samples cleanly:** **0 divergences**, R̂ ≤ 1.01, BFMI 0.84–0.98.
3. **The treatment effect:** `b_bar = 0.702` log-odds (94% HDI 0.195 to 1.165) → odds **×2.02**. Chimps favour the prosocial-*side* lever about 2:1 — a handedness effect more than altruism (Silk et al.'s own conclusion).
4. **The correlation, reported honestly:** `rho = −0.123` (94% HDI **−0.889 to 0.681**), P(rho<0) = 0.61 — the data are nearly silent on it; the LKJ(2) prior dominates.
5. **PPC is excellent:** per-actor predicted pull rates match observed to ≤ 0.016 — the random intercepts absorb the huge between-actor spread (0.35 to 1.00).
6. **Correlation barely helps prediction:** correlated vs independent `elpd_diff = 0.23 ± 0.11` (2.1 SE), max Pareto-k̂ 0.21 — a tiny gain, exactly what you expect when `rho` is weakly identified.
7. **SBC certifies the sampler:** rank histogram for `b_bar` flat, chi-square p = **0.942** → calibrated (100 sims, trimmed 250/250 sampler).

## 3. Common mistakes
- **Reporting `rho` as if it were precise.** With 7 actors the correlation is dominated by the LKJ prior — always show the full HDI.
- **Using a centered MvNormal** and ignoring divergences; the non-centered `chol @ z` form is the safe default.
- **Calling the prosocial-side effect "altruism."** It is a left-side preference; the design confounds side with treatment.
- **Skipping the independent-effects comparison.** Without it you cannot say whether modelling the correlation actually mattered (here: barely).
