# Lessons Report — Project 18: Cherry-Blossom Timing (B-splines)

**Author:** Truong Van Thien, PhD · **Date:** 12-06-2026 · **Model tag:** Opus48

## 1. The concept taught
Fitting a **smooth, non-linear time trend** with a **B-spline basis expansion** — the practical, O(n)
alternative to an exact Gaussian process (which is O(n³) and would blow the runtime budget on ~800 points).
You also meet **HSGP**, the low-rank approximate GP, as the bridge to genuine GP models.

## 2. Workflow lessons from this run (with the actual numbers)
1. **Recovery on a curve:** simulating from a known smooth function + Normal(0,6) noise, an 18-knot spline
   covered ≥ 80% of the truth with its 90% ribbon and recovered σ̂ ≈ 6.1 (truth 6.0) before any real data.
2. **A basis expansion is just a linear model in disguise:** 15 quantile knots → a 827×17 design matrix, then
   ordinary `mu = a + B·w`. All 17 weights mixed well (R̂ = 1.00, min ESS ≈ 836, 0 divergences).
3. **The trend is real and non-linear:** mean bloom-day ≈ 103.3 before 1100 vs ≈ 100.4 after 1900 (~2.9 days
   earlier), and LOO prefers the spline over a straight line by **52.7 elpd (5.3 SE)** — decisive.
4. **The fit is honest about its tails:** PPC mean/sd/max p ≈ 0.50–0.64, but the earliest blooms are slightly
   under-predicted (min p = 0.09) — a small, documented residual.
5. **Calibrated and prior-robust:** SBC ranks were uniform (chi-square p = 0.90) and power-scaling left σ
   unchanged to three decimals.

## 3. Common mistakes
- **Reaching for an exact GP** on hundreds of points — correct in spirit, fatal for runtime. Splines/HSGP scale.
- **Extrapolating a B-spline** beyond the data; outside the knot range the basis gives no honest uncertainty
  (the HSGP is better there).
- **Too many knots** → overfitting wiggles; too few → missing real structure. Let LOO arbitrate.
- **Forgetting the missing `doy` rows** (388 of 1215) — modelling them as zeros or dropping silently both bite.

## 4. Try it yourself
1. Sweep `num_knots` ∈ {5, 15, 30}; watch wiggliness and LOO — what controls overfitting?
2. Fit `doy ~ temp` directly and quantify days-per-°C; compare its LOO to the time-only spline.
3. Use the HSGP model to predict 2025–2050 bloom days with calibrated uncertainty, and explain why a
   B-spline cannot do this honestly.

## 5. Self-check
1. Why is a B-spline basis O(n) where an exact GP is O(n³), and when does that distinction decide the model?
2. What do the 17 spline weights *mean*, and why does local support prevent polynomial-style tail blow-up?
3. LOO favours the spline by 52.7 ± 9.9. Is that decisive? How do you judge it?
4. The SBC chi-square p was 0.90. What would a ∪- or ∩-shaped rank histogram have told you?
5. Why is extrapolating this spline to year 2100 a bad idea, and what model would you use instead?
