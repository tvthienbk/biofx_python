# Lessons Report — Project 07: UCBadmit (Binomial GLM), Simpson's paradox revealed

**Author:** Truong Van Thien, PhD · **Date:** 12-06-2026 · **Model tag:** Opus48

## 1. The concept taught
The **aggregated Binomial GLM** `admit ~ Binomial(applications, p)` with a logit link, and the most
famous lesson in applied statistics: **conditioning on a confounder can reverse a conclusion**
(Simpson's paradox). P02 estimated one pooled rate; P07 adds department as an **index variable** and
the apparent male advantage evaporates.

## 2. Workflow lessons from this run (with the actual numbers)
1. **Recovery first:** simulating with `b_male = -0.1` and six known department log-odds recovered the
   gender coefficient (mean **-0.121**, 90% HDI -0.202..-0.035, truth inside); R̂ = 1.00.
2. **The paradox in the raw data:** pooled admit rates are **women 0.304 vs men 0.445** (men look
   favoured), yet department A admits **82.4% of women vs 62.1% of men**, and B is 0.680 vs 0.630.
3. **The coefficient flips:** `b_male` = **+0.609** log-odds (OR **1.84**) in the gender-only model but
   **-0.103** (OR **0.90**) once department is added — a change of **-0.711**. P(b_male<0) rises from
   0.00 to **0.91**. This IS Simpson's paradox, made quantitative.
4. **The PPC proves the misfit:** the gender-only model's 94% predictive band covers only **0.17** of
   the 12 observed cells (χ² = 433); dept+gender covers **0.92** (χ² = 8.2).
5. **LOO is decisive but approximate here:** `elpd_diff` = **427 ± 151** (2.8 SE) favours dept+gender,
   stacking weight 1.00 — but max Pareto-k̂ = **1.02 > 0.70** because each aggregated cell is a
   high-leverage observation. Honest reporting: the direction is unambiguous; a disaggregated Bernoulli
   or `reloo` would give an exact elpd if needed.
6. **Prior is irrelevant:** power-scaling leaves E[b_male] at -0.103/-0.103/-0.102 across α.

## 3. Common mistakes
- **Reporting the pooled gender gap** as if it were a causal effect — the textbook error this dataset exists to refute.
- **Trusting raw LOO numbers** for aggregated Binomial models without checking Pareto-k̂.
- **Using dummy contrasts** instead of an index variable, which makes the per-department intercepts and their priors harder to reason about.

## 4. Try it yourself
1. Give each department its own gender slope `b_male[dept]`. Which department genuinely favours women? (A.)
2. Fit a hierarchical `a_dept ~ Normal(mu, tau)` and measure each department's shrinkage.
3. For each department, compute P(female rate > male rate). Where is the evidence strongest?

## 5. Self-check
